# Project volume first, then price the tails

For the 2026 NFL season, the evidence supports building player-prop projections from **opportunity, which repeats from week to week, and regressing conversion, which mostly does not**. Year over year, targets per route run (TPRR) correlates about **0.63–0.64**, target share has an R² of 0.40, QB rush attempts **0.89** and RB carries 0.61. Catch rate (R² 0.11), yards per target (R² 0.03), YPC (0.37), RYOE (0.33) and TD rate (R² 0.01) are mostly noise. Volume should come from team plays × shrunk PROE × a spread term worth about **0.5 pass-rate points per point of spread**. Distributions need to be simulated, because yardage is right-skewed and zero-inflated: a line set near the median makes a mean-based projection lean over. The only reasonably documented NFL market pattern is the same one Prop-Lab already grades against: **unders on low receiving and rushing lines**, led by RB receiving-yards unders (+108 units in 2020–22). The best public prop record shows edges shrinking (ETR ROI fell from 22.1% in 2021 to 0.5–14% since). Anytime-TD markets carry **25–45% summed hold** and best-price gaps of **4–10 probability points**, so shopping and Kalshi reference prices matter more there than modeling finesse. On the MLB side, the K model v0 is missing two things that pull in opposite directions. The first is a **postseason hook**: modeling batters faced as a pitch budget instead of Normal(20.5, 3.5) raises Misiorowski's P(under 6.5) from 56% to about 67%. The second is a **park K factor**: American Family Field runs about 12% above average for strikeouts, and that factor alone brings v0 down to about 44–50%, close to Pinnacle's 47%. Tonight's paper under also **fails two of Prop-Lab's own Rules guards** (blend check +0.3 pts against a 2-pt minimum; shrunk edge 2.8 against a 3-pt minimum), so it should be logged as WATCH. Before anything is used, the Scout has to re-verify three slate facts. Public sources label the Oct 25–26 games **Week 7, not Week 8**. Every line in this report dates from Oct 20–22. The sources also disagree on at least one total (NE@CHI: 50.5 at Complex vs 42.5 in the vault's DraftKings quote). Evidence labels: **[S]** sourced, **[I]** inference, **[C]** computed for this report, **[G]** gap.

## Receiving props: routes and target earning are stable, catch and YAC luck regress

Two independent stability studies agree once their units match. 4for4 (WRs with 30+ targets in consecutive seasons, 2017–23) reports year-over-year r of **0.75 for slot rate, 0.68 receptions per game, 0.65 aDOT, 0.64 TPRR and 0.56 YPRR**, but only 0.48 for YAC per reception and 0.22 for on-target catch % ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/node/173504)) [S]. Sharp Football's R² values (targets per route 0.392, team target share 0.401, air yards per target 0.379, YPRR 0.281, catch % 0.113, yards per target 0.028, TD per target 0.008) convert to r ≈ 0.63 for TPRR and 0.53 for YPRR ([Sharp Football Analysis](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/wide-receiver-stats-that-matter-fantasy-football-2024/)) [S/C]. SumerSports puts YPRR at 0.51 but **expected YPRR, which is built from personnel and down/distance, at 0.67**, and treats actual minus expected as descriptive, not predictive ([SumerSports](https://sumersports.com/the-zone/revisiting-yards-per-route-run/)) [S]. An older 2007–12 regression gives the shrinkage directly: **TPRR(N+1) = 0.062 + 0.671 × TPRR(N)**, against a 0.28 slope for yards per target ([Football Perspective](https://www.footballperspective.com/yards-per-route-run-yards-per-target-and-targets-per-route-run/)) [S, dated]. Taken together, the engine should derive targets from routes × TPRR and lean on the player's depth profile for yards per target, regressing his own catch rate and YAC heavily. 4for4's year-over-year r of **0.01 for route rate** is a different case. Route participation is set by the current depth chart and personnel, not by talent, so it should come from this week's role [I].

One result changes how injury news should flow through the engine. Across 2017–18, the share of a team's targets that returned was **uncorrelated (r = 0.03) with changes in remaining players' targets per snap**. Players gained targets by gaining snaps, not by earning targets at a higher rate ([Razzball](https://football.razzball.com/do-returning-rb-wr-te-benefit-when-a-teams-returning-target-share-is-low/)) [S]. When a WR1 or TE1 sits, the engine should hold each player's TPRR roughly fixed and re-solve route participation from the new depth chart. The replacement inherits routes at his own, usually lower, TPRR. Some of the lost volume also leaks to RB/TE checkdowns, non-targets and a lower pass rate [I]. Assigning vacated targets in proportion to existing shares, which is the NFL version of the bug fixed in NBA v1.1, would overstate the WR2 [I]. Backup QBs need the same caution. PFF's 2025 case studies run in both directions: Jefferson saw double-digit targets in all five Wentz starts, and Olave had all three of his 100-yard games with Shough ([PFF](https://pff.com/news/fantasy-football-how-do-backup-qbs-affect-skill-player-production)) [S]. No league-wide study exists [G], so a flat "backup QB → fade receivers" haircut is not supported. Model the QB change through three channels (team YPA, depth profile, target concentration), and let the tracker test whether concentration is real [I].

### Coverage shells are a small, shrunk interaction, not a regime switch

Two-high safety looks reached **42.0% of dropbacks in 2025**, the highest in the Next Gen Stats era (32.9% in 2018). Every defense exceeded 30%, average safety depth was a record-shallow 12.0 yards, and blitz rate is **39.7% from single-high vs 10.0% from two-high** ([NFL.com/NGS](https://amp.nfl.com/news/the-coverage-that-turned-the-seahawks-into-super-bowl-winners-and-is-taking-the-nfl-by-storm)) [S]. At the receiver level, Reception Perception success rate against man coverage is stable (**r = 0.72**), against zone much less so (0.42), and each predicts the other poorly (0.18–0.26), which suggests man and zone skill are separate traits ([SumerSports](https://sumersports.com/the-zone/examining-wide-receiver-metrics/)) [S]. The evidence stops there. Success rate is not TPRR, and no public study shows a receiver's man/zone TPRR split predicting his weekly target share [G]. The engine form that fits this evidence is a multiplier, TPRR_game = TPRR_base × [p_man × r_man + (1 − p_man) × r_zone], where r is the player's split ratio shrunk hard toward 1 (less shrinkage for the man split) and p_man is the opponent's regressed rate [I]. A worked example shows how small the effect is [C]. Take a receiver whose raw man ratio is 1.15, shrunk to 1.075, and whose zone ratio is 0.95, shrunk to 0.9875. Moving him from a league-typical man rate of 35% to a man-heavy opponent at 50% raises his TPRR multiplier from 1.018 to 1.031, about **+1.3%**. That is worth roughly 0.1 targets. Coverage shells belong in engine v1.5, not in the Sunday build, and they are a poor tracker hypothesis unless the split is extreme. 2026 team man/zone rates are also paywalled [G].

### Price receiving markets from the simulated median and CDF

Receiving outcomes are skewed and zero-inflated. An RB with a 75% chance of zero catches and a 25% chance of 10 has a mean of 2.5 and a median of 0. Dalvin Cook averaged 3+ catches yet had ≤ 2 in 8 of 14 games in 2020 ([Advanced Sports Analytics](https://advancedsportsanalytics.substack.com/p/getting-ready-for-nfl-props-and-sports)) [S]. A WR averaging 65 yards can be a bad bet over 50, longest-reception overs at 23.5 hit only 50–55% for star WRs, and pace acts as a multiplier on longest reception ([FTN](https://ftnfantasy.com/nfl/betting-nfl-props-using-math-week-1)) [S]. Half-point lines on discrete counts matter: in one 2025 tout record, **24.2% of losing reception props lost by exactly one catch**, against 15.6% in earlier seasons ([Walter Picks](https://walterpicks.beehiiv.com/p/full-season-nfl-player-prop-report-card)) [S, small sample]. The engine therefore simulates targets (negative binomial built from dropbacks × routes × TPRR, with target share drawn as a Beta random effect) and catches (per-target catch probability from air yards plus shrunk CROE). Each catch's yards are drawn as air yards plus a right-skewed YAC term. Longest reception falls out of the same simulation as P(longest > L) = 1 − E[F(L)^N] [I]. No public fit of the receiving-yards distribution or of the mean-minus-median gap by position exists [G]. The 5–15% WR1 gap in the notes is an estimate to calibrate on 2023–25 nflverse data before it is trusted [I].

## Rushing and QB props: PROE and script set the volume, efficiency barely persists

Sharp Football's 2026 offensive PROE through about Week 6 runs from **Dallas +7.7, Carolina +7.3, Cincinnati +7.3 and Pittsburgh +6.4** at the pass-heavy end to **Miami −10.6, the Chargers −7.9 and Atlanta −7.2** at the run-heavy end. Defensive PROE faced identifies pass funnels (**Detroit +7.1, Atlanta +6.0, Washington +5.4, Tampa Bay +5.3**) and run funnels (**San Francisco −4.8, Dallas −4.0, Minnesota −3.7**) ([Sharp Football Analysis](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/stats-nfl/nfl-pass-rate-over-expected/)) [S]. Six-game PROE is noisy: Seattle was +8.5% through Week 5 of 2025 and then ran more than expected for the rest of the season ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/how-play-calling-tendencies-affect-fantasy-football)) [S]. No week-to-week stability coefficient was found [G], so a roughly **50/50 blend with the prior season and coaching history at Weeks 6–8** is the working assumption [I]. The spread effect is real but modest. Across about 1,500 team-games, pass rate correlated with spread at 0.47, worth **about +0.5 percentage points per point of spread**. That means a 3-point underdog throws about 2 more passes than a 3-point favorite, and favored QBs beat DFS expectations by +0.3, which suggests the market over-penalizes favorites' passing ([FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/why-the-spread-matters-less-than-you-think-for-nfl-dfs/)) [S]. The tails matter more than that average slope. Rachaad White averaged 12.4 carries in Tampa Bay losses vs 19.3 in wins, and one bettor's unders lost in Week 15 of 2023 when blowouts inflated the winner's RB and passing volume ([Stealing Lines](https://stealinglines.substack.com/p/finding-a-flaw-in-the-process-best)) [S]. Older 4for4 data show league rush rate at **40.5% neutral, 51.5% leading by 8+, and 28.0% trailing by 8+**, with coach-level lead/trail sensitivity ranging from 20.4 to 37.7 points ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/fantasy-football/how-play-calling-tendencies-affect-fantasy-football)) [S, 2016-era]. So the engine should draw a final margin for each simulation, map it to pass rate, and draw plays from that, rather than plugging in the mean spread [I].

For RBs, **rush attempts per game repeat at r = 0.61, but yards per attempt only 0.37 and RYOE per attempt 0.33**, while RB receptions per game (0.70) and receiving yards per game (0.68) are stickier than rushing efficiency ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2024/preseason/most-predictable-running-back-stats)) [S]. Within a season, split-half RYOE correlates at only 0.17. In one example, a single 91-yard TD made up nearly all of a back's surplus ([StatsbyLopez](https://statsbylopez.netlify.com/post/assessing-running-back-performance-using-distributions/)) [S]. Run-defense rank persists year to year (r = 0.529) while pass-defense yards allowed shows "no meaningful" correlation, and even the run schedule is worth only about 3.5 yards per game after uncertainty ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/according-to-the-data-strength-of-schedule-part-2-18085)) [S]. The design follows directly: carries come from team rush attempts × carry share, and YPC is regressed 60–65% toward a blend of team blocking and opponent run defense [I]. Pass-defense "DvP" yardage gets no weight. The common rule that an RB2 absorbs 60–75% of vacated carries is unsourced and has to be fitted [G].

For QBs, **rush attempts per game repeat at 0.89 and rush yards per game at 0.86**, against 0.62 for pass attempts, 0.42 for CPOE (0.51 once drops, throwaways and screens are filtered), 0.32 for YPA and 0.26 for INT rate ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2024/preseason/most-predictable-quarterback-stats); [SumerSports](https://sumersports.com/the-zone/cutting-through-noise-to-increase-cpoe-stability/)) [S]. Sacks belong to the QB more than to the offensive line ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/are-nfl-quarterbacks-responsible-for-sack-quality)) [S]. The 2024 league pressure-to-sack rate was **18.69%**, with Josh Allen about 19 sacks under expectation and Caleb Williams 23 over ([Trap Sheet](https://trapsheet.substack.com/p/new-stats-self-sack-score-and-sacks)) [S]. That yields a concrete pass-attempts adjustment: attempts = dropbacks × (1 − sack share − scramble share). A sack-prone or scramble-heavy QB converts fewer of PROE's dropbacks into attempts, which is a candidate attempts-under mechanism [I].

Weather is the one context factor with usable numbers. Average team passing falls from **227 yards and 7.3 YPA at 0–7 mph wind to 208 at 12–14 mph and 206 at 20+**. Measurable rain cuts passing by about 15%, and rain games went under 57.4% of the time (343 games). At 15–19 mph, totals already sit about 1.5 points lower, so books adjust totals ([Sharp Football Analysis](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/betting/nfl-weather-betting/amp/)) [S]. Those figures imply passing-yard haircuts of **−8.4% at 12–14 mph, −7.0% at 15–19 and −9.3% at 20+** relative to calm conditions [C]. The step starts around 12 mph, not 15 [I]. Deep receivers and longest-reception props should take larger cuts, since 25+ yard completion rate drops from 5.6% to about 5.0% [S]. Snow and cold were not quantified [G].

## Anytime TDs and market patterns: a goal-line model plus aggressive price shopping

Rushing TDs are concentrated near the goal line. Since 2010, **57.4% have come from inside the 5, 74.0% from inside the 10, and 43.4% from the 1- or 2-yard line**, and a carry from the 1 is worth about six times one from the 19 ([Sharp Football Analysis](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/nfl-red-zone-stats-expectations-running-backs-2025/)) [S]. TD rate per target is the least stable receiving stat (R² 0.008) [S, above]. The model therefore takes team implied points (home = (total − home spread)/2), converts them to expected offensive TDs and splits those into rush and pass pools. Each player's λ is his inside-10/inside-5 carry share or end-zone/red-zone target share of the relevant pool, with QB sneaks as a separate competitor, and **P(anytime) = 1 − e^(−λ)**, or a negative binomial for goal-line backs [I]. As a worked example [C/I], Houston at about 26 implied points (from HOU −9.5, total 42.5) yields roughly 2.6 TDs if about 70% of points come from TDs. Assume a 45% rush share and a 60% inside-10 share for an RB1, plus 0.08 receiving TDs: λ ≈ 0.79, or **P ≈ 55%** (fair price about −120). Every one of those shares is an assumption to be fitted from nflverse. TD regression should shrink toward opportunity-based expected TDs (xTD), not toward a league average. Sharp's TDs-over-expected tables are paywalled, so Prop-Lab has to compute xTD itself [G/I].

What sets TD markets apart is their pricing structure. Summed anytime-TD boards held **26.7–47.1% on yes-only books** (DraftKings 38%, FanDuel 33%) against 4.1% on a two-way SuperBook market. First-TD average hold ranged from 6.9% (SuperBook) to 65.5% (2022 playoffs) ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nfl/best-sportsbooks-first-touchdown-anytime-td-props)) [S, dated 2022]. In Week 1 of 2026, best-vs-worst price gaps on the same player ran **9.7 points (Jefferson, +120 to +180), 6.8 (Nacua) and 5.9 (St. Brown)**. Kalshi beat the book quote on 59% of 1,247 comparisons, with a **median 1¢ spread on TD contracts** (8¢ on receiving yards). The taker fee is 0.07 × p × (1 − p), and resting maker orders avoid it ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-touchdown-props)) [S]. For Prop-Lab, Kalshi's TD mid is the best available "sharp devig" reference for anytime TDs. Under Rules §4, a yes-only book with more than about 10% hold is never a benchmark [I]. A TD bet should clear the Kalshi-derived fair price at the best executable book, and first-TD markets should be ignored outside near-fair books [I].

The documented NFL market pattern is narrow. In PFF's blind-betting data (2020 through November 2022), **RB receiving-yards unders returned +108.09 units, receiving-yards unders on 10–19-yard lines +70, rushing-yards unders on 0–9 lines about +30, and passing-yards unders +14.06**. Receiving-yards overs were "really difficult" to beat ([PFF](https://www.pff.com/news/nfl-betting-2022-midseason-player-prop-performance-review)) [S]. Newer evidence is weaker. A 2025 tout record had unders at 34-22 against overs at 95-105 ([Walter Picks](https://walterpicks.beehiiv.com/p/full-season-nfl-player-prop-report-card)) [S, selection-biased]. One bettor's unders went 111-64 in 2022 and then 12-24 from Week 7 of 2023 ([Stealing Lines](https://stealinglines.substack.com/p/finding-a-flaw-in-the-process-best)) [S]. ETR's self-reported prop ROI fell from **22.1% (2021) to 9.5%, 0.48%, 14.09% and 6.03%**, with lines moving "within a few seconds to 30 seconds" of release ([ETR](https://establishtherun.com/etr-in-season-nfl-props-faq/)) [S]. The mechanism behind the under lean is mean-above-median skew plus public demand for overs [I]. It is plausible and consistent with the data, but it is not measured at closing prices for the NFL the way SickFade measured it for the NBA [G]. No 2025–26 evidence was found on primetime efficiency, the speed of inactive-news repricing, stale 4pm lines, alt-ladder mispricing or pick'em-app lag [G]. Those are tracker questions, not facts.

## NFL engine v1: reuse the NBA skeleton, swap minutes for team volume

NBA v1.2 already provides the scaffolding the NFL needs: a volume driver, shrunk rates, market anchoring to implied team totals, one shared draw per simulation, distributions fitted from logs, and the Rules §3 guards (shrunk edge ≥ 3, blend ≥ 2, more than 10-pt disagreement = WATCH, stale-price path). The NFL changes the volume driver and the stat chains.

The `qs` format was removed in 2026, so the engine should read parquet ([nflreadr NEWS](https://cloud.r-project.org/web/packages/nflreadr/news/news.html)) [S]. Two points are unconfirmed [G]. For external inputs, Sharp's PROE page supplies team rates, Kalshi supplies TD reference prices, and Open-Meteo or the NWS API supplies kickoff wind and gusts [I].

## MLB World Series: the K model needs a hook, a park factor and the Rules guards

### Strikeouts and outs: what the evidence changes in v0

The K model v0 draws BF from Normal(20.5, 3.5), shrinks pooled K% toward league with n0 = 150, applies log5 against the lineup, and samples Binomial(BF, Beta). Five findings bear on it. **First, postseason hooks are short.** Misiorowski went 4.0 and 4.1 IP on 182 pitches across two playoff starts (**21.8 pitches/IP**). Opponents' foul rate rose from 18.5% to 28%, and his whiff rate fell from **37.3% to 22.3% overall and from 40% to 16% on a fastball he throws more than 60% of the time** ([ats.io](https://ats.io/mlb/rays-vs-brewers-world-series-game-1-player-props-10-23-2026/)) [S]. He was handled carefully after a midseason layoff ([WMTV](https://www.wmtv15news.com/2026/07/21/brewers-remaining-careful-with-jacob-misiorowski-he-returns-layoff/)) [S], and Brewers starters totaled 17 IP across four starts while their bullpen allowed 3 ER in 32 IP ([ABC/ESPN](https://abc7.com/post/what-is-real-isnt-2026-mlb-playoffs-far/19940270/)) [S]. **Second, there is no sharp third-time-through cliff.** Once batter and pitcher quality are adjusted for, Brill, Deshpande and Wyner find decline is continuous, so K probability should decay smoothly by batter number ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06724)) [S]. **Third, K% stabilizes fast.** Carleton puts the 0.70 reliability point at about **70 BF** ([Baseball Prospectus](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/20516/baseball-therapy-should-i-worry-about-my-favorite-pitcher/)) [S]. For an established pitcher, that argues for shrinking the postseason sample toward his own regular season with weight n/(n + 70), not shrinking the pooled sample toward league. For Misiorowski the two methods happen to agree: 37/107 gives about **35.1%**, against v0's 34.8% [C]. **Fourth, arsenal features carry information that K% alone does not.** Velocity and movement clusters predict K% with **test MAE of 2.94 points (adjusted R² 0.42)** ([Martin/SABR](https://global-uploads.webflow.com/5f1af76ed86d6771ad48324b/5f6d38971aa75c2f6af77911_Predicting-Major-League-Baseball-Strikeout-Rates-Update.pdf)) [S]. No public study benchmarks an arsenal-vs-lineup model against log5 for single-game K counts [G]. **Fifth, ABS is a small effect.** Through May 3, fielders' challenges added 181 strikeouts and batters' erased 130, and out-of-zone called strikes fell from 6.69% to 5.09% ([The Analyst](https://theanalyst.com/articles/abs-automated-ball-strike-challenge-system-growing-in-2026-mlb-season)) [S]. The net is about **+0.05 K per team-game** [C], so the right response is to halve pre-2026 umpire K factors, not to add a K% term [I].

One input v0 omits matters a lot. **American Family Field ran about 12% above league average in K% (through 2024)** and was 2nd-hardest for singles ([Shepherd Express](https://shepherdexpress.com/sports/brewers-on-deck/american-family-fields-unusual-offensive-environment)) [S, secondary source]. Re-running v0's simulation shows that the hook and the park factor pull in opposite directions, and that both are large relative to the edge [C]:

| Scenario (Rays lineup K% 19.1% as in v0) | Mean BF | Mean K | P(under 6.5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| v0: talent 34.8%, BF ~ N(20.5, 3.5) | 20.5 | 6.27 | **56.0%** |
| + pitch-budget hook (88 ± 10 pitches ÷ 4.8 P/PA) | 18.4 | 5.64 | 67.0% |
| v0 + full AmFam K factor (×1.12) | 20.5 | — | 43.6% |
| v0 + half factor (×1.06, home starts already in RS K%) | 20.5 | — | 49.6% |
| Half factor + hook at 4.8 P/PA | 18.4 | — | 61.2% |
| Half factor + hook at 4.3 P/PA (regular-season efficiency) | 20.6 | 6.68 | 48.9% |
| Talent shaded to 32% for the whiff collapse, half factor + hook | 18.4 | — | 70.0% |

Pinnacle's devigged fair under is **47.0%**. Once the park is in the model, **the whole disagreement rests on one assumption: does his postseason 4.8 P/PA and ~88-pitch leash persist?** That is the same question ats.io's model answered "yes" (No on 7+ at 55.8%) and the market answered "partly." The Rules check is decisive regardless [C]. At FanDuel +104, break-even is 49.0%. The blend (0.75 × 47.0 + 0.25 × 56.1) is **49.3%, only +0.3 pts over break-even against the required 2**, and the shrunk edge (0.4 × 7.0) is **2.8 against the required 3**. Under Rules v2 this is **WATCH, not PAPER**. Any v1 version that adds the hook pushes the model-vs-sharp gap past 10 points, which is also WATCH. Two further cautions [G]. v0's Rays K% vs RHP (19.1%) was not found in a source during this research and should be verified on Savant. And the G1 result was unknown at writing.

The upgraded model (k_model_v1) is a **PA-by-PA simulation** [I]. Each PA draws an outcome (K, BB/HBP, ball-in-play out, hit) and a pitch count conditioned on that outcome. The starter is pulled when cumulative pitches exceed a postseason hook threshold (drawn per pitcher from his last two or three starts and manager pattern) or when a score/TTO rule fires. Talent K% is reliability-blended toward the pitcher's own regular season and then blended about 50/50 with an arsenal estimate: pitch-type usage by batter hand × pitcher whiff on that pitch × lineup whiff on that pitch, each regressed. A smooth per-PA decay applies for batters 19–27, a venue K factor applies after neutralizing the home park, and the catcher's challenge skill is a small add-on (Dingler 15/17, Smith 23 overturns) [S/I]. Because outs and Ks come out of the same simulation, outs props and K+outs same-game parlays are priced coherently. When foul rate and P/PA jump, **outs fall faster than Ks**, so outs unders are the purer expression of a hook thesis [I]. The remaining World Series games split by venue. G1–2 and G6–7 are at AmFam, where roof status goes into the Scout fields. G3–5 are at Tropicana Field, a fixed dome that is pitcher-leaning and in the bottom half for HR ([MLB.com](https://www.mlb.com/rays/news/rays-have-home-field-advantage-with-hot-start-at-tropicana-field)) [S]. Rays starters have worked 3.2–5.1 IP in this postseason, and ALCS MVP Freddy Peralta has both started and relieved ([Pitcher List](https://pitcherlist.com/mlb-lcs-news-moments-rays-move-on-to-world-series-set-to-face-brewers/)) [S], so every Rays outs prop carries a fat left tail.

### Batters: PA by slot, regressed rates, outcome-specific park factors

PA per game started falls by about 0.1 per lineup slot, **from 4.65 (leadoff) to 3.77 (9th)**. With substitutions the gap widens sharply, and platoon players and weak defenders are pulled most ([FanGraphs](https://fantasy.fangraphs.com/buying-generic-plate-appearances-by-lineup-spot)) [S, 2016, pre-universal DH]. In postseason games, stars are rarely pinch-hit for, so regulars sit near the "started" column. Low scoring trims team PA, and home hitters lose PA when the bottom of the 9th is skipped [I]. Per-PA rates should blend expected and actual stats: the best weights are **BA 73/27, SLG 59/41 and wOBA 65/35 (x/actual)**, and the blended wOBA beats either input alone (RMSE .0378 vs .0385/.0403) ([FanGraphs](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/yes-hitter-xstats-are-useful/)) [S]. K% stabilizes at about 150 PA, BB% at 200, HR rate at 300 and ISO at 550, and BABIP never does within a season ([Grantland/Carleton](https://grantland.com/the-triangle/fantasy-fiesta-when-can-we-trust-the-numbers/?print=1)) [S]. Platoon splits regress with **1,000 PA vs LHP for left-handed hitters and 2,200 for right-handed hitters** ([FanGraphs](https://blogs.fangraphs.com/estimating-hitter-platoon-skill)) [S]. Batter-vs-pitcher history gets a weight of zero: 60 targeted PA do not outweigh 1,500 general PA ([RotoWire, citing The Book](https://www.rotowire.com/baseball/article/the-sabers-edge-batter-vs-pitcher-stats-23835)) [S].

Environment carries real signal this October. **The 2026 postseason hit .212 through 30 games, on pace for the lowest OPS since 1983, with 31% of team-games at two runs or fewer**, and relievers averaged about 98 mph ([ABC/ESPN](https://abc7.com/post/what-is-real-isnt-2026-mlb-playoffs-far/19940270/)) [S]. AmFam needs **outcome-specific factors** (about +14% HR, −3% runs, singles and doubles suppressed, K up), not a single run factor ([Shepherd Express](https://shepherdexpress.com/sports/brewers-on-deck/american-family-fields-unusual-offensive-environment)) [S]. That leans against hits and H+R+RBI overs and toward HR and walk "yes" prices at Milwaukee [I]. Domes neutralize the roughly +2% fly-ball HR chance per °C ([Eos/Dartmouth](https://eos.org/articles/climate-change-knocks-it-out-of-the-park)) [S]. On distributions, hits follow a Poisson-binomial over a random number of PA, and TB comes from a per-PA 0/1/2/3/4 outcome mix. At a .200 hit rate and 3.9 PA, P(≥1 hit) is about 58%, against about 70% for a .230 leadoff hitter at 4.65 PA [C]. A few tenths of a PA or 20 points of hit rate move the fair price 3–6 points. No study of MLB batter-prop market bias was found [G], so every batter hypothesis below needs its own controls.

## Pre-register N1–N10 and M1–M8 with matched controls, and expect slow verdicts

The tracker's test stays the same: net = (hit − p_market)_hypothesis − (hit − p_market)_control, clustered by game, with the control on the **same side, same stat, price within ±10 cents, same slate, and no mechanism**. Two changes are needed [I/C]. First, `SIDE_BIAS` in `tracker.py` is NBA-sourced (SickFade). The NFL and MLB have no closing-price equivalent [G], so new arms must log controls from the first night and should not fall back on the ±1.5 prior. Second, sample size. At p ≈ 0.5, 150 entries give ±4.1 points. A 13-game NFL Sunday/Monday slate realistically yields 20–40 entries per hypothesis family, so **reaching 400 entries takes 10–20 weeks, longer than the 12 weeks left in the regular season** [C]. ## Conclusion

Both sports point to the same design rule, and it is sharper in the NFL than in the NBA: **project the denominator (plays, routes, carries, batters faced), regress the numerator, and price from the simulated distribution rather than the mean**. The less obvious result is how much of any apparent edge sits in a single structural input. The Misiorowski under goes from a fair coin to a 67% favorite depending on one hook assumption, and from 56% to 44% depending on one park factor that v0 never included. A model that reports a single number without that sensitivity is reporting confidence it doesn't have. In practice, model upgrades should be judged by whether they **shrink disagreement with sharp prices for explainable reasons** (the park factor did) before they are trusted to widen it (the hook does).

The NFL calendar also changes the strategy. Twelve regular-season weeks cannot settle ten pre-registered arms on hit rate, so this season's learning has to come from CLV and log-loss, from pooling arms around the one mechanism with real evidence behind it (skewed low-line unders), and from markets where structure alone creates edges: TD prices at 25–45% hold set against a 1¢ Kalshi spread. Building the Sunday engine as volume plus shares, without routes or coverage, gives up little, because the sourced effects of those refinements are small (about ±1–3% for shells) next to the cost of acting on stale slate facts, which is the larger risk this weekend.
