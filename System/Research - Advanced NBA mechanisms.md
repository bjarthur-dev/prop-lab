# Rebuild NBA props around volume, opportunity and minutes

For 2026-27, NBA player-prop projections should rest on a few evidence-backed ideas rather than narratives. **Shot volume and shot location are stable; make rates (especially 3P%) are mostly noise and need heavy regression.** **Defenses control where opponents shoot, not whether the shots fall.** **Rebounds and assists are opportunity × conversion, and the conversion half regresses.** **Minutes are the biggest lever, and they depend on rotation structure and on the distribution of final margins, not on a straight-line spread rule.** In the market, the one documented tilt is that **unders lose less than overs at closing prices** (-3.4% vs -9.2% ROI on about 396k NBA props). Neither side beats the vig. That matters for Prop-Lab because the zero-stake tracker grades every hypothesis against the devigged market probability, so an under hypothesis gets a built-in tailwind of roughly 1.5 points and an over hypothesis a matching headwind. H3 ("big-favorite star → points under") is the weakest of the three as framed. Public evidence that heavy-favorite stars underperform is thin and team-dependent. At spreads of 7.5–9.5 the extra blowout risk is worth about half a minute, or 1.5–2.5 points of probability, and books build props from minutes projections, so it is almost certainly priced. Testing engine v1 also exposed two mechanical problems that matter more than any new feature. **The blowout rule is mostly undone by the minutes normalization** (a stated 0.875-minute loss at -9.5 becomes about 0.26). **Vacated minutes flow to the stars, not to the replacement**, so the engine pushes a 34-minute star to the 38-minute cap and gives a backup center only +2.6 minutes when a 30-minute starter sits. That works directly against H2. Fix those, rebuild threes, rebounds and assists as opportunity × regressed conversion, add matched control arms to the tracker, and pre-register five new hypotheses (H4–H8) that target mechanisms the market is less likely to price. Evidence labels used throughout: **[S]** sourced, **[I]** inference, **[C]** computed here, **[G]** gap.

## Shot volume is signal and make rates are noise, so rebuild points and threes from attempts

The best-established result in this research is that **where and how often a player shoots is far more stable than how often the shots go in**. In the "Meta-Analytics" study, over **50% of between-player variance in single-season 3P% is chance**, making it the least stable and least discriminative common metric. FT% is very stable over time, and empirical-Bayes shrinkage toward a career average improves 3P% on both counts ([Franks et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.09830)) [S]. Kostya Medvedovsky fitted padding values that predict rest-of-season results from year-to-date data across about 750K player-game rows. His optimal in-season 3P% padding is **about 240 league-average attempts**. Blackport's older 750-attempt figure answers a different question (the reliability-0.5 point), so the two do not conflict ([Medvedovsky](https://kmedved.com/2020/08/06/nba-stabilization-rates-and-the-padding-approach/)) [S]. BBall Index's padded "stable stats" show what this means in practice: Max Christie's 42% on 62 attempts padded to 37%, and he shot 36% the next year ([BBall Index](https://www.bball-index.com/creating-more-predictive-stats/)) [S]. Team 3P% correlates only **0.42** between halves of a season ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/nba/2021/2/12/22279459/nba-make-miss-3-point-shooting)) [S].

Shot context moves expected make rates by several points, and it is measurable. In 2021-22, catch-and-shoot threes went in at **36.4% versus 32.8% for pull-ups**, wide-open threes above 38% versus about 29% for tight ones, and corner threes above 38% versus under 35% above the break ([Best Ball Stats](https://bestballstats.com/2022/09/18/true-shooting-ability-separating-shooting-talent-from-shot-circumstances/)) [S]. Cleaning the Glass's zone baselines (garbage time excluded) put the rim at 66.3%, short mid at 44.2%, long mid at 41.7%, corner three at 38.9% and non-corner three at 35.6% ([CtG](https://cleaningtheglass.com/stats/league/shots)) [S]. The practical consequence [I] is that a usage spike that turns catch-and-shoot attempts into pull-ups lowers expected 3P% even though the player's talent hasn't changed. This interaction matters directly for H1 beneficiaries.

The binomial arithmetic shows why recent shooting tells you so little [C]. A 7-attempt shooter at a true 37% has a per-game SD of about **1.28 made threes (about 3.8 points)**, and over 10 games the SD of his observed 3P% is about 5.8 percentage points, so a 43% stretch is within one SD of a 37% true talent. With 240 attempts of padding toward his own prior, a career 36% shooter who opens 27-for-60 (45%) projects to **37.8%**: about 80% of the hot start is noise. No study was found showing that prop lines over-react to hot shooting as opposed to volume [G]. That makes it a testable hypothesis (H4 below), not a known edge.

Engine v1 handles none of this. `fg3m` is a single per-minute rate shrunk toward the **league** per-minute rate with 300 minutes of padding (`config.SHRINK_MIN`). That pulls a high-volume specialist toward an average player's volume while barely regressing his make rate, which is the reverse of what the evidence supports. The fix splits volume from conversion. 3PA per minute updates fast, with light padding of about 100 minutes [I]. 3P% is padded with about 240 attempts toward the player's own multi-season prior, and that prior is itself padded toward league average with about 750 attempts [I, built from the Medvedovsky and Blackport figures]. 3PM is then drawn as Binomial(3PA, p) with 3PA from a negative binomial, rather than as a negative binomial on makes. Points should eventually be decomposed the same way, as minutes × FGA/min × zone mix × padded zone FG% + minutes × FTA/min × FT% [I]. The stability order to build around is minutes > shot volume and mix > FT% and FTr > rim FG% > mid-range FG% ≈ 3P% [I, consistent with sources].

## Defenses choose where opponents shoot, so replace DvP with regressed frequency-allowed indices

The matchup evidence points one way. Team-level opponent shot frequencies at the rim, mid-range and three correlate **0.55–0.67 year to year**. Rim FG% allowed is the only zone *efficiency* with visible persistence ([Best Ball Stats](https://bestballstats.com/2021/12/27/the-keys-to-a-successful-nba-defense)) [S]. Opponent 3P% allowed correlates only **0.12** between halves of a season. The 2014-15 Blazers allowed 29.7% before the All-Star break and 37.4% after ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/nba/2021/2/12/22279459/nba-make-miss-3-point-shooting)) [S]. College data agree: early-season gaps in opponent 3P% kept only about 9% of their separation later in the season, while gaps in opponent 3PA rate kept about half ([KenPom](https://kenpom.com/blog/3point-defense-should-not-be-defined-by-opponents-3p/)) [S]. Individual defenders work the same way. In the spatial "Counterpoints" model, frequency suppression and efficiency suppression are distinct traits: Roy Hibbert ranked 1st in near-rim efficiency suppression but 161st in frequency suppression. Defender effects also needed shrinkage to a prior variance only **10–20%** of shooter variance, and they improved out-of-sample prediction only modestly ([Franks, Miller, Bornn & Goldsberry](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0231)) [S].

So defense-vs-position (DvP) tables should go. They mix pace, opponent shooting luck, schedule and fuzzy position labels. FantasyLabs showed one team ranking 8th-toughest against point guards on raw DvP but 24th after a salary adjustment ([FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/why-dvp-data-is-misleading/)) [S]. No study was found that measures DvP's out-of-sample predictive power for props [G]. The replacement [I] is a set of opponent *rate* indices per 100 possessions: 3PA rate allowed, rim-attempt frequency allowed, FT rate allowed, OREB% and DREB%, and forced TOV%. Each is regressed **30–50% toward 1.0** for frequencies and about **90% toward league average** for 3P% allowed. Applied to a 7-3PA shooter, a ±10–15% opponent 3PA-rate index becomes about **±0.3–0.6 3PA** after regression [I]. That is small, but it matters against threes lines set at x.5.

Pick-and-roll coverage is real but smaller for props than film discussion implies. In 2020-21 the league ran drop on 58% of ball screens, switch on 18% and blitz on about 3%, but blitzes were concentrated on stars: Curry 20%, Beal 15%, Lillard about 14%. Lillard scored **0.98 points per chance against blitzes versus 1.2 against drop** ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/nba/2021/5/17/22439801/nba-play-in-playoffs-steph-curry-damian-lillard)) [S]. A hand-chart of SGA found 1.43 PPP against drop-family coverages versus 0.96 against switch/blitz-heavy looks ([Low Man Help](https://lowmanhelp.substack.com/p/part-ii-sgas-best-and-worst-pick)) [S, small sample]. Switching directionally lowers handler shots and raises handler passes ([Ben Everett](https://medium.com/@beneverett/switch-5-part-1-what-tracking-era-numbers-reveal-about-the-nbas-hottest-trend-53dea6df3e09)) [S]. Because blitzes are only 14–20% of a star's pick-and-rolls, an 18% per-chance penalty works out to **a few percent of his points over a whole game** [I], unless a team blitzes at playoff rates. No public team-by-team coverage table exists for 2025-26 or 2026-27 (Synergy and Second Spectrum hold it) [G], so scheme should be a later, proxy-based feature. Zone is similar: NBA.com's new detection model puts it at **about 2.5% of possessions league-wide** this season ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/detecting-zone-defense-measuring-impact)) [S]. Denver's heavy zone against OKC in the 2025 WCF cut SGA's usage below 30% and his TS% from 64% to 57% while raising his assist rate ([The F5](https://thef5.substack.com/p/zone-defense)) [S]. Ignore zone unless a team uses it more than about 10% of the time [I].

For timing [I]: classify schemes from last season plus coaching and personnel changes through about mid-November. Start blending in opponent 3PA rate, rim rate, OREB% and TOV% after 10–15 games, and never trust opponent FG% by zone. Team efficiency settles to within 5% after about five games and team 3P% after about 300 attempts in an Italian-league analog ([Basketball Analytics Lab](https://basketballanalyticslab.substack.com/p/finding-stability-when-early-season)) [S, non-NBA]. No NBA stabilization study exists for opponent OREB%, forced TOV% or coverage frequencies [G].

## Minutes decide most props, and engine v1's minutes logic undercuts H1, H2 and H3

Minutes are "extremely stable" because coaches decide them ([Medvedovsky](https://kmedved.com/2020/08/06/nba-stabilization-rates-and-the-padding-approach/)) [S]. A PointsBet trader confirmed that book odds "start with a minutes projection" ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/how-blowouts-hurting-prop-bettors-nba-postseason)) [S]. That cuts both ways. Minutes are where the model must be best, and any minutes effect the market can see from the spread (like H3) is likely already in the line.

### Testing engine v1 on a synthetic ten-man rotation exposed two structural faults [C]

`project_game` subtracts the blowout loss from starters and then renormalizes to 241 team minutes, spreading the gap back across every free player in proportion to his minutes. Because starters hold about 60% of those minutes, **they recover most of what the blowout rule just took**. At a -9.5 spread the stated loss is 0.875 minutes, but the 34-minute star actually falls only to **33.74** (-0.26). At -15 the stated 2.25 becomes about -0.7. The second fault is worse. When a 30-minute starter is ruled out, the same proportional redistribution sends the stars to the 38-minute cap (34 → 38.0, 33 → 37.85) and gives the 18-minute backup only **+2.6 minutes (to 20.6)**. `min_overrides` can only cap minutes (`np.minimum`), never raise them. In real rotations the replacement absorbs most of the vacated minutes, so the engine **systematically under-projects H2 targets and inflates the stars' minutes in H1 games**. The star's projected points in the test rose 11% from minutes alone, which is the wrong channel. Points are then multiplied by a uniform team factor `f_pts`, which amounts to redistributing vacated usage with exponent k = 1 and no efficiency penalty.

### Usage redistributes toward creators and costs efficiency

The research consensus is that absorbed usage costs efficiency. Dean Oliver estimated about **0.6 points per 100 possessions of ORtg per +1% usage**, Eli Witus about **1.25**, and Neil Paine's blended slopes are about **1.67 for low-usage and 0.83 for high-usage** players ([Neil Paine](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/optimizing-the-95-rockets)) [S]. Usage concentrates when no second creator remains: Caris LeVert ran at 41.3% usage over 261 minutes with Brooklyn's creators out ([Stokastic](https://www.stokastic.com/articles/dfs-strategy/nba-dfs-usage-rate-strategy)) [S]. The structural replacement [I] runs in a fixed order:

1. Allocate the absent player's minutes by position and depth chart.
2. Split his on-court usage share V among teammates in proportion to u_i^k with **k ≈ 1.5–2** (start there and calibrate on league-wide absence games).
3. Cut each absorber's points per usage-possession by the Paine slope for his usage tier.
4. Shift ball-handling assists to the next primary handler.

Worked example [C]: a 20%-usage wing who absorbs +3 usage points gains about 15% in shot volume but loses about 2.5–5 ORtg points. His net points rise roughly 11–13%, not the 15% a proportional bump gives. With/without splits under about 200–300 shared minutes should only lightly update this structural prior [I]. Team scoring falls when a star sits, so not every teammate's points rise.

### Blowouts belong in a margin distribution, and H3 is mostly priced

Cleaning the Glass defines garbage time as Q4 with a margin of 25+ (12:00–9:00), 20+ (9:00–6:00) or 10+ (under 6:00), and two or fewer starters on the floor ([CtG](https://cleaningtheglass.com/stats/guide/garbage_time)) [S]. No public study gives starter minutes lost per point of spread [G]. The direct evidence for heavy-favorite star unders is weak. A FantasyLabs trend study of double-digit spreads found a "very, very marginal positive" overall and called it probably not meaningful. Results varied by team: Curry averaged 44.72 DraftKings points against 47.93 implied, while Spurs players were positive ([FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/dfs-trend-of-the-day-2316/)) [S]. To size H3, I modeled final margin as Normal(spread, 12.5) [I: the SD is an unsourced prior to re-fit]. Starters were assumed to lose 6 minutes when the final |margin| is 20 or more and 3 minutes at 15–19 [I: illustrative]. The incremental expected loss versus a pick'em comes out to **+0.43 min at -7.5, +0.55 at -8.5 and +0.67 at -9.5** [C]. That is close to what v1 intends (0.375–0.875) and much smaller than the narrative implies, because pick'em games also blow out about 11% of the time. For a 35-minute, 25-point star against a 24.5 line, removing 0.6 minutes lowers P(over) from 49.1% to 47.2%, and 1.0 minute lowers it to 45.7% [C, engine-style NB simulation]. So all five H3 entries tonight sit at spreads where the true effect is worth about 2 points of probability, and that is the kind of effect a minutes-based book prices.

The upgrade [I] is to draw a final margin in each simulation from the spread and an SD fitted from the user's own logs, then apply a **coach-specific pull curve**, P(starters pulled | margin, time left), estimated from play-by-play as the first moment no more than one starter is on the floor. The same margin draw should drive an overtime branch: about **6.2–6.4% of games went to OT** in 2013-16, adding about 20 total points ([RotoGrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/articles/banking-on-overtime-1710010)) [S, dated]. OT is likelier at small spreads [I]. It should also drive a late-game free-throw branch for close games, where the leading team's inbound guard plausibly gains 2–4 FTA. That would be worth about +0.3–0.8 expected points at small spreads and almost nothing at big ones [I, no NBA quantification found; G]. If H3 has a real edge, it lives in coach pull speed and in alt-line tails, not in the spread threshold itself.

### Coaching, rest and schedule context for 2026-27

Six teams have new head coaches: Splitter (CHI), May (DAL), Jenkins (MIL), Mosley (NOP), Sweeney (ORL) and Nori (POR). May has signaled game-plan-dependent, deeper rotations ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/closer-look-new-coaches-2026-27)) [S]. Their minutes priors should be shrunk harder toward league shape for 10–15 games [I]. The Participation Policy and the 65-game awards rule remain in force ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/nbpa-rips-participation-policy-65-game-rule/)) [S], and enforcement is active. The Pacers were fined $100,000 for sitting Siakam and two other starters, and the Jazz **$500,000 for pulling Markkanen and Jaren Jackson Jr. before the fourth quarter** ([CBS Sports](https://cbssports.com/nba/news/nba-anti-tanking-rule-changes-next-season)) [S]. Rest now shows up mostly as softer minutes rather than DNPs [I]. That weakens the "bad team, star sits late" story the user wants to move past. On schedule, teams in 2025-26 won about **44% on the second night of a back-to-back versus 51% with rest**, with net rating down about 1.5. The Nets, Celtics and Mavericks each have 16 back-to-backs in 2026-27 ([KRDO/Stacker](https://krdo.com/stacker-basketball/2026/10/15/surviving-the-schedule-how-back-to-back-games-will-hurt-or-help-nba-teams-in-2026-27/)) [S]. Foul-trouble benching may be rational because players in foul trouble play worse, though coaches vary ([Sloan / Maymin et al.](https://sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/how-much-trouble-is-early-foul-trouble)) [S]. Model it as a left-tail minutes risk for high-foul-rate bigs [I]. The new officiating emphases on push-offs, illegal screens and off-ball contact ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25513499-explaining-nba-rule-changes-2026-27-season-including-nba-cup-bracket-format)) [S] could raise early-season foul and FTA rates. Watch the league FTA rate for 2–3 weeks before trusting 2025-26 FT rates [I]. League fouls already jumped 1.3 per game to 19.9 in 2025-26 ([SI](https://www.si.com/nba/four-stats-that-will-define-2026-27-nba-season-teams-that-do-them-best)) [S].

## Rebounds and assists are opportunity times conversion, and conversion regresses

### Rebounds depend on the lineup and the opponent

NBA.com tracking splits rebounding into chances, deferred chances, adjusted chance % and contested versus uncontested rebounds ([NBA.com glossary](https://www.nba.com/stats/help/glossary)) [S]. A Bayesian lineup model in the *Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports* shows individual rebound rates are a property of all ten players. Raw rates rank players poorly (Kendall's tau 0.51 on defense, 0.41 on offense), and adding Gobert in place of Vanderbilt lowers Towns' predicted rate from **21.3% to 20.2%**. That is cannibalization measured directly. About **5.6% of defensive and 25.1% of offensive rebounds are team rebounds** that no player gets ([JQAS](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jqas-2023-0097/html)) [S]. Teams rebounded better with DeAndre Jordan and Andre Drummond *off* the floor ([Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2015/03/how-good-a-marginal-rebounder-are-you-really.html)) [S, hypothesis-level]. For matchups, elite rebounders moved about ±1 RPG against the worst and best rebounding opponents but less with opponent missed-shot volume, so **opponent rebound % matters more than opponent misses** ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/basketball/article/numbers-game-how-missed-shots-impact-rebounds-31759)) [S]. The "long misses mean long rebounds" story found no team-level support ([Northwestern](https://sites.northwestern.edu/nusportsanalytics/2018/02/28/do-longer-shot-attempts-mean-longer-rebounds)) [S, crude method].

The environment has shifted. League offensive rebounds hit **11.4 per game in 2025-26, the highest since 2012**, led by Houston (15.0), Portland (14.1), Detroit (13.1), Phoenix (13.0) and Charlotte (12.8) ([SI](https://www.si.com/nba/four-stats-that-will-define-2026-27-nba-season-teams-that-do-them-best)) [S]. Teams that get back on defense can cut their own offensive-rebound probability by more than half ([Sloan](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/to-crash-or-not-to-crash)) [S]. v1 scales rebounds by (game total / league total)^0.5 and nothing else. The replacement [I] splits DREB and OREB:

- **E[DREB]** = E[MIN] × opponent misses per minute × (1 − opponent OREB%_reg) × (1 − 0.056) × player DREB share
- **E[OREB]** = E[MIN] × own misses per minute × (1 − opponent DREB%_reg) × (1 − 0.251) × player OREB share

Each share comes from lineup-aware on/off rates. It is adjusted down when a second big or an elite-rebounding wing shares the floor, and up when one is out. That last adjustment is the mechanism H2 is really betting on.

### Assists depend on teammates finishing

**Potential assists** (passes leading to a shot within one dribble) measure the opportunity, and `PlayerDashPtPass` returns per-receiver pass counts with the receiver's FG2/FG3 off those passes ([nba_api docs](https://github.com/swar/nba_api/blob/master/docs/nba_api/stats/endpoints/playerdashptpass.md)) [S]. Conversion depends heavily on teammates. In 2016-17, 8 of the top 75 passers by assists per potential assist were Spurs, while the Bulls, bottom five in FG% and 3P%, had one player in the top 90. +10 MPG outweighed every other factor ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/basketball/article/numbers-game-the-impact-of-trades-on-assists-31551)) [S]. Assist rate and OREB% are the two rate stats most independent of role when usage changes sharply ([Neil Paine](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/which-offensive-rate-stats-stay-the)) [S]. The model [I] is **E[AST] = E[MIN] × PotAST/min × expected conversion**, where conversion is the shot-quality-weighted, regressed FG% of the actual receivers. The "luck term" (actual AST − PotAST × expected conversion) should mean-revert over 5–10 games because receivers' short-sample 3P% is noise. v1's `AST_EXP = 0.8` on the team points factor gets the direction backwards when a shooter is out. The passer's assists should *fall* because his best finisher is gone, even while his points rise. No league-wide AST/PotAST average or reliability figure was sourced; compute it from `leaguedashptstats` Passing [G].

### Steals, blocks and combos

Blocks are stable and discriminative ([Franks et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.09830)) [S], and stocks overs are heavily juiced (Mobley 1.5 at -160/+120) ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/pra-pa-stocks-prop-betting)) [S]. Combo lines track the sum of their component lines, and blindly betting combos showed no edge ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/pra-pa-stocks-prop-betting)) [S]. v1 already simulates combos jointly through a shared minutes draw. The missing piece [I] is a conditional points-assists correlation (negative for primary creators), estimated from each player's residuals, so PA and PRA alt lines get the right variance. No sourced correlation coefficients exist [G].

## The market's one documented bias favors unders, so the tracker needs control arms

SickFade's aggregate of **396,482 graded NBA props** shows flat bets at closing prices returning **-9.2% on overs and -3.4% on unders** ([SickFade](https://sickfade.com/overs-vs-unders)) [S, grade B-: undated, method thin]. At -110 those returns imply win rates of about **47.6% for overs and 50.6% for unders** against the 50/50 a symmetric devig assigns [C]. A practitioner NFL log showed an unders edge collapsing mid-2023 as books adjusted ([Stealing Lines](https://stealinglines.substack.com/p/finding-a-flaw-in-the-process-best)) [S, grade C], so the bias may be shrinking. On market structure, books pull lines within about 15 seconds of news while pick'em apps lagged by minutes in 2023 ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2023/preseason/how-win-money-playing-pick-em-sites)) [S, dated]. FanDuel and Caesars are the most efficient domestic prop books, and DraftKings, Caesars and MGM reportedly share a prop source ([Establish The Run](https://establishtherun.com/understanding-the-current-ecosystem-of-nfl-player-props/)) [S, NFL context]. Holds run about 4–6% on main props and higher on secondary and exotic markets ([Wizard of Odds](https://wizardofodds.com/article/player-props-understanding-the-math-behind-the-lines/)) [S, grade C]. No NBA-specific evidence was found on star versus role-player pricing, primetime, head-to-head history, revenge games or last-5 streaks [G].

The 2026-27 rules changed what can be bet. On Oct 16 the NBA told partner books to drop first-stat and opening-tip markets, "miss a shot" props, **unders on players not expected to play more than a few minutes**, and **multiple unders on the same player in a parlay** ([AP via NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/10/16/some-wagers-certain-parlays-no-longer-can-be-offered-to-bettors-nba-tells-sportsbooks)) [S]. Covers reported the low-minute threshold as 12 minutes ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/nba-bans-certain-bet-types-ahead-of-new-season-oct-16-2026)) [S]. Prediction markets reportedly fall outside these rules ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/?p=282553)) [S, headline only]. Kalshi lists multi-threshold player ladders ([SI](https://www.si.com/prediction-markets/reviews/kalshi-nba)) [S], which makes it a natural venue to compare against the engine's full distribution.

The under bias has direct consequences for the tracker [I/C]. Grading H3 (unders) against `p_market` builds in a roughly **+1.5-point tailwind**, and grading H1 and H2 (overs) builds in a **-1.5-point headwind**, before any mechanism is involved. A positive H3 result would be indistinguishable from "unders lose less." Every hypothesis therefore needs a **matched control arm**: same side, same stat, same book, a price within ±10 cents, logged the same night, without the mechanism. The test statistic is (hit − p_market)_hypothesis − (hit − p_market)_control. Sample size also matters [C]. At p ≈ 0.5 the standard error of a hit rate is 0.5/√n, so 150 entries give **±4.1 points**. Detecting a 5-point edge at two standard errors needs about **400 entries**, and a 3-point edge about 1,100. Entries from the same game (tonight, Davis and George in TOR@WAS, Doncic and Reaves in LAC@LAL) should be clustered by game. Track two faster secondary metrics alongside hit rate: CLV against the devigged close, and log-loss of the engine's `p_model` versus `p_market`.

## Upgrade engine v1 to v1.5 component by component

The doc's own sequencing still holds: calibrate the base on the real-data backtest first, then add features one at a time and keep only those that improve log-loss against the close. Within that rule, the changes the notes support are listed below.

| Component (file) | v1 now | v1.5 change | Parameters / formula | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Pre-register tracker v2 with five new mechanisms and matched controls

Each definition below is meant to be frozen before tip, with inputs computed by code from data as of the morning, the price and timestamp at entry, and a control row logged alongside. The current three hypotheses should be tightened. **H1** should split into H1a (the single highest-usage remaining creator, where concentration k > 1 predicts the largest share) and H1b (all other beneficiaries). It should also record minutes since the news broke, because the documented edge is staleness ([4for4](https://www.4for4.com/2023/preseason/how-win-money-playing-pick-em-sites)), not the story. **H2** should require a confirmed start and a projected minutes increase of at least 8 over the player's season average, and should log the remaining co-big's status. **H3** should be kept only as a control-matched test at |spread| ≥ 10 plus a coach pull-speed tercile once it is measured. As framed tonight, at 7.5–9.5, its expected mechanism effect is about 2 points of probability and largely priced.

P1 exists so the user can show with their own data that the popular tool adds nothing.

## Drop these narratives and pull these endpoints

The narratives to drop:

- **DvP rank tables** ([FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/why-dvp-data-is-misleading/)).
- **Opponent 3P% allowed and opponent FG% by zone** ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/nba/2021/2/12/22279459/nba-make-miss-3-point-shooting)).
- **"Hot shooter keeps it going"** ([Franks et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.09830)).
- **Primary-defender head-to-head samples**, which are confounded by assignment ([Hoopbag](https://edoardohoopbag.substack.com/p/defense-part-1-what-do-their-most)).
- **"Long misses mean long rebounds"** without player-level verification.
- **Raw on/off under about 250 shared minutes** [I].
- **Zone or blitz adjustments** for teams below playoff-style frequencies.
- **Referee crews as a primary edge**: crew-height effects were about 3.6% more fouls with no effect on total points ([WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/onlyagame/2014/11/08/paul-gift-nba-referees-foul)), so treat them as a tiebreaker.
- **Altitude**, which is not separated from ordinary home-court advantage ([NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/is-impact-of-denvers-altitude-fact-or-fiction)).
- **Late-season "star sits"** on tanking teams, now that $500,000 fines exist.
- **Head-to-head history, revenge games and last-5 streaks**. No study supports them [G], and the earlier audit found tonight-style bets built on them.

## Conclusion

The pattern across all five research threads is that **opportunity persists and conversion regresses**: attempts versus makes, potential assists versus assists, rebound chances versus rebounds, and shot frequency allowed versus percentage allowed. Most popular prop narratives read the noisy half. A model built on the stable half, with the noisy half padded toward priors, gets most of what public research supports. Whether any of it beats books that also start from minutes projections can only be answered by the user's own controlled record. The test results also suggest the cheapest gains are not new features at all. Two lines of normalization logic currently undercut the engine's own blowout rule and its handling of replacement starters, the exact mechanisms the tracker is testing.

The tracker should also change how it asks its question. "Does H3 hit more than the market implies?" can't be answered without a control, because unders are documented to lose less regardless of mechanism. The better question for every arm is whether this mechanism beats its own matched baseline, and by more than the roughly ±4-point noise floor of a 150-entry sample. Framed that way, the next few hundred zero-stake entries can settle which narratives to discard. H4–H8 point that test at conversion luck and persistent opponent frequencies, where the evidence suggests mispricing is most plausible.
